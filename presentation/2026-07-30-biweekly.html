The compact takeaway

Organ specialization improves prediction whether the system uses:

  • the revealed organ;
  • one automatically chosen specialist; or
  • a weighted blend of specialists.

The automatic chooser preserves nearly all of the benefit without being told the organ.

Meaning

With a fixed data budget, keeping target-organ data is better than replacing it with another organ.

This does not mean all sharing is bad. The next test keeps all target-organ data and asks whether another organ adds useful information.

Next step · representation-first pivot

Measure the expert signal before engineering how to share it

The nine-run diagnosis found no reliable organ-pair rule. The active audit now asks whether the validated specialists make reproducible gene-level corrections across independently trained models.

Result

Raw sharing is unstable

No helpful pair passed across all nine model-and-training combinations; two pairs were reliably harmful.

Interpretation: selecting a lucky run or pair would not produce a defensible rule.

Active now

Map stable expert programs

Compare each organ specialist with the general model, a generic adapter, and random specialists across three independent models and held-out donors.

Evidence: gene-level correction similarity, rank agreement, top-gene overlap, and donor-bootstrap intervals.

Decision gate

Predict sharing—or broaden

If at least 4 of 8 organs pass every reproducibility gate, freeze a prospective sharing predictor. Otherwise examine tissue sites, pathways, states, and continuous residual structure.

Keep fixed: organ experts and the general model remain benchmark controls either way.

Target end product: a reproducible rule that predicts which biological domains should share training information—and a model that can safely decline to share.
