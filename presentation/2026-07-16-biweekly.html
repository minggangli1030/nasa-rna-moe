The earlier “no headroom” result was not a valid MoE test

What July 9 appeared to show

A fixed blend helped slightly, but choosing among specialists added almost nothing.

Old conclusion: expert quality may be too weak to justify routing.

What was wrong

  • Raw TPM entered models trained on log1p(TPM).
  • Blend weights were fitted on the reporting cohort.
  • The gene-mean baseline came from test data.
  • A hard selector was treated as the routing ceiling.
  • Large GEO studies dominated sample-weighted averages.

This sprint’s main contribution: freeze a leakage-resistant, study-aware evaluation before interpreting MoE headroom.

06
Interpretation

What Stage 1 now establishes — and what it does not

Supported

Species specialization is useful

  • Hard routing already gives most of the gain.
  • Soft routing adds a smaller refinement.
  • A blind expression gate recovers the route.
  • Scaling 5k → 20k increases adaptive headroom.
Still controlled

Not yet a complete systems claim

  • Three full experts store more parameters than one model.
  • The pooled V3 model had species-local batches.
  • Current results are reconstruction, not downstream spaceflight classification.
  • Organ specialization must be tested independently.

Active control: the pooled model is being retrained with globally shuffled cross-species batches; frozen evaluation will start automatically after completion.
